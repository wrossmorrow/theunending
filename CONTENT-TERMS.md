## An honest note on what can actually be licensed

The images in this project were generated by a diffusion model from prompts.
Under current U.S. law this probably means **no one holds copyright in the raw
output files themselves**.

The requirement that a copyrightable work have a human author was upheld by
the D.C. Circuit in *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, and the Supreme Court declined to
review that decision on March 2, 2026. The Copyright Office's position is that
prompts operate as instructions conveying unprotectable ideas, because
present-day tools do not give the prompter sufficient control over the result.
No court or agency has yet drawn a line for how much human contribution is
enough; *Allen v. Perlmutter* may begin to.

So the grant above is made in two registers, and it is worth being clear about
which is which:

- **The prompt texts, the written content, and the selection, sequencing and
  arrangement** of images into sets and posters are human-authored, and the
  license operates on them normally.

- **The raw generated image files** are another matter. There, the license is
  better read as a statement of intent than as an enforceable restriction. If
  it turns out there is nothing to license, then nothing here grants or
  withholds anything, and the request stands on its own terms: please don't
  sell these.

Nothing in this file is a representation that any particular element is or is
not protected by copyright, and nothing here is legal advice.
